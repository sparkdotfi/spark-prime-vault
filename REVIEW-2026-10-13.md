# spPRIME (`SparkPrimeVault.sol`) accounting review

Branch `lucas/refactor-vault` @ `807739f`, reviewed 2026-10-13 against a mainnet fork (block 26148600, spUSDC = `0x28B3…a43d`, impl `0x1b99…f455`).
PoC tests: `test/Review.t.sol` (`forge test --match-contract ReviewTest`, 10/10 pass, committed on local branch `review/poc-tests`).

Severity counts (after Lucas's answers, 2026-10-13 23:00): **Critical 0 · High 1 · Medium 2 · Low 4 · Informational 9**.

Answers received: deposit-queue spUSDC is intentionally takeable; instant redemptions are gone for good and deposit cancels should get the same treatment as withdrawals (planner-handled); guardian cancels must refund `request.owner`; only spUSDC is ever custodied (USDC never sits in the contract); ERC-7540 is dropped; redeem fee stays charged up front; `chi` keeps accruing during pause (VSR can be set to RAY instead).

## Model of the accounting (as implemented)

- Shares are priced by an index: `assets = shares * chi / RAY`, `chi` compounds at `vsr` per second via `drip()`. `totalAssets()` is purely notional (`totalSupply * chi / RAY`); nothing on-chain relates it to real backing.
- Every asset the vault receives goes straight into spUSDC (`requestDeposit` line 366). The vault's only on-chain backing is its spUSDC balance; active capital is whatever the PAU has `take()`n (spUSDC shares, line 294).
- Deposit queue entries are denominated in spUSDC shares. A fill converts spUSDC shares → USDC (spUSDC's `convertToAssets`) → spPRIME shares (`convertToShares`) and mints to the recipient; the spUSDC stays in the vault and becomes general backing (lines 647–654).
- Redeem queue entries are denominated in spPRIME shares, escrowed in the vault, fee burned up front (lines 429–442). A fill burns the shares and calls spUSDC `withdraw(assets)` directly to the recipient (lines 688–692).
- There are no claim steps, no instant redeem path, and no idle-USDC accounting. Capacity caps `totalSupply` in shares and may be set below it.
- Loss handling: `setChi` lowers the index while paused (line 327).

## Findings

### F-1 · High · Guardian (or owner) can redirect a cancelled request to any address
**Location:** `cancelDepositRequest` lines 393–411, `cancelRedeemRequest` lines 447–464.
**What happens:** Both cancel functions take a caller-supplied `recipient_` and pay the refund (spUSDC redeemed to USDC, or escrowed spPRIME shares) to it. The GUARDIAN_ROLE may cancel any request, so a guardian key can cancel a user's queued deposit or redeem and send the proceeds to itself. The comment on line 392 and the README both say refunds go to the owner.
**Impact:** Direct theft of every queued deposit and every escrowed redeem by a role that is meant to be low-trust (pause + compliance cancel).
**Fix:** Refund to `request_.owner` unconditionally; if the owner needs a custom recipient, only honour `recipient_` when `msg.sender == request_.owner`.
**PoC:** `test_F1_guardianCancelDepositRedirectsFunds`, `test_F1_guardianCancelRedeemRedirectsShares`.

### F-2 · Medium (was High; ring-fencing is not intended) · `cancelDepositRequest` reverts once the queue backing has been taken
**Location:** `take` lines 294–303, `availableLiquidAssets` line 604, `_processRedeemQueue` line 669.
**What happens:** `pendingDepositSavingsShares` tracks the spUSDC owed to queued depositors, but neither `take()` (limit is the full spUSDC balance) nor `_processRedeemQueue` (limit is `spUSDC.maxWithdraw(vault)`) subtracts it. Both can spend queued depositors' spUSDC. Afterwards `cancelDepositRequest` reverts for the affected depositor (spUSDC `redeem` fails), and `processDepositQueue` will still mint shares against backing that has already left.
**Impact:** Queued depositors cannot exit until the PAU returns spUSDC or capacity opens; the README invariant "queued deposit shares are strictly locked" is false; the rebalancer has no on-chain guard and must compute the safe amount off-chain. If several depositors are queued, an early cancel takes a later depositor's backing (last one out is stuck).
**Fix (revised):** Since `take()` is meant to spend this spUSDC and cancels are meant to get the same treatment as withdrawals, make deposit cancellation planner-handled: `cancelDepositRequest` should move the entry (or its spUSDC amount) into a planner-settled refund path rather than calling spUSDC `redeem` synchronously, and `processDepositQueue` should not assume the spUSDC is still present. At minimum, document that a cancel can revert until the planner restores liquidity.
**PoC:** `test_F2_takeSpendsQueuedDepositBacking`, `test_F2_processRedeemQueuePaysRedeemersWithQueuedDepositBacking`.

### F-3 · Medium · `_processDepositQueue` always stops after the first request (`maxRequests_` is inert)
**Location:** line 658: `if (requestSavingsShares_ != 0) break;`
**What happens:** The partial-fill check tests the memory copy of the request's *original* share amount (always non-zero at that point) instead of `request.shares` (the redeem loop at line 694 does it correctly). The loop therefore breaks after one request every time, and `depositHead` is left pointing at the filled entry.
**Impact:** One queued deposit per `processDepositQueue` call regardless of `maxRequests_` and capacity; batch processing does not work. Head still advances correctly on the next call (the zeroed entry is skipped as "cancelled"), so no funds are at risk.
**Fix:** `if (request.shares != 0) break;`
**PoC:** `test_F3_processDepositQueueOnlyFillsOneRequestPerCall`.

### F-4 · Low · Dust fills consume the depositor's spUSDC and mint zero shares
**Location:** lines 644–654.
**What happens:** When `remaining_` is a few wei of spUSDC shares (capacity that converts to ≥1 spUSDC share but <1 spPRIME share once `chi > RAY`), the loop deducts `savingsShares_` from the request and from `pendingDepositSavingsShares`, then mints `shares_ == 0`. The value is ~1e-6 USD per call, but it is a silent transfer from the head depositor to the vault and emits `Deposit(…, assets=1, shares=0)`.
**Impact:** Dust-level loss for the head request; noisy events; any invariant of the form "shares minted > 0 per fill" fails.
**Fix:** `if (shares_ == 0) break;` before mutating state (or round the capacity → spUSDC conversion so `remaining_` is zero whenever it would mint nothing).
**PoC:** `test_F4_dustFillMintsZeroShares`.

### F-5 · Low · Zero and dust requests bloat the queues for free
**Location:** `requestDeposit` line 353 (`minDeposit` defaults to 0), `requestRedeem` lines 422–436.
**What happens:** `requestDeposit(0, …)` pushes a zero-share entry (spUSDC `deposit(0)` succeeds). `requestRedeem(1, …)` with a 1% fee burns the single wei as fee and pushes a zero-share "ghost" entry. Each entry costs the attacker only gas, but every later `process*Queue` call must iterate past them (bounded by `maxRequests_`, so it is throughput griefing, not a hard DoS).
**Impact:** Queue bloat, rebalancer gas, pointless events; `cancel*Request` on a ghost reverts with `no-request`.
**Fix:** Require `assets_ != 0` / `queuedShares_ != 0`, and set non-zero `minDeposit`/`minWithdraw` at deploy.
**PoC:** `test_F5_zeroDepositRequestsBloatQueue`, `test_F5_tinyRedeemRequestsBecomeGhostEntries`.

### F-6 · Informational (was Medium; USDC is never custodied) · No path for USDC that lands in the contract
**Location:** `availableLiquidAssets` line 604; no `depositToSavings`-style function; `forceApprove` at line 241 is only used by `requestDeposit`.
**What happens:** Liquidity is measured solely as `spUSDC.maxWithdraw(vault)`. If the PAU (or anyone) returns USDC rather than spUSDC, it sits in the vault uncounted, cannot be used to pay redeems, and cannot be moved into spUSDC except through a proxy upgrade.
**Impact:** Operational foot-gun; returned USDC is stuck until an upgrade.
**Fix:** Lucas confirms capital returns as spUSDC and USDC never sits here. Consider a REBALANCER `sweepToSavings()` anyway as a cheap safety valve for mistaken USDC transfers.

### F-7 · Low · Cancels are blocked while paused, `take()` is not
**Location:** `cancelDepositRequest` / `cancelRedeemRequest` (`whenNotPaused`), `take` (no modifier).
**What happens:** During a pause users cannot retrieve queued deposits or escrowed shares, while the PAU can still pull spUSDC (including, per F-2, the deposit-queue backing). Spark's requirement that claims work while paused has no equivalent here because fills pay out directly, but the intent (users can always reach what is already theirs) would argue for cancels to work while paused.
**Fix:** Decide (Q3). If pause is meant to freeze everything for a loss event, consider pausing `take` too; if users should be able to exit, drop `whenNotPaused` from the cancels (deposit cancel is safe to allow while paused since it never touches `chi`).

### F-8 · Low · Redeem fee is charged on request and kept on guardian cancel
**Location:** lines 431–436, 457–461.
**What happens:** The fee is burned when the request is made and not refunded on cancel. A guardian compliance-cancel therefore costs the user the fee. Combined with F-1 the guardian can also redirect the remainder.
**Fix:** Only charge the fee on fill, or refund it on cancel (track it in the `Request`).

### Informational

- **I-1** `redeemInstantly_` (line 427) is computed and unused; instant redemption was removed in `811a768` but Sam's requirements still describe instant withdrawals drawing from the spUSDC sleeve (Q2). `processRedeemQueue` is REBALANCER-only, so every exit now depends on Spark.
- **I-2** The burned fee reduces `totalSupply` with no change in backing, so fee value accrues to vault equity (which `take` can extract) rather than to a fee receiver. Fine if intended; the README says "retained by the vault".
- **I-3** `UNPAUSER_ROLE` is declared but `unpause` is DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE. Role cleanup TODO at line 49 applies.
- **I-4** `minWithdraw` is checked on gross shares; the net payout after fee can be up to 1% below it.
- **I-5** `processDepositQueue(maxSavingsShares_, …)` is denominated in spUSDC shares while the redeem counterpart is in spPRIME shares; the README says `maxAssets`. Pick one unit for operator safety.
- **I-6** spPRIME transferred to the vault itself (`transfer(address(vault), x)`) is permanently stuck; `balanceOf[vault] == pendingRedeemShares` only holds absent donations. Harmless, but block `to_ == address(this)` in `transfer`/`transferFrom` if the invariant is wanted.
- **I-7** `totalAssets()` is purely notional. There is no on-chain solvency check and no way to recognise spUSDC yield to holders other than `setVSR`; losses are recognised only by a manual `setChi`. Pending redeem shares sit inside `totalSupply`, so a redeem queue blocks deposit capacity until the netting TODO (line 48) lands.
- **I-8** `README.md` and `docs/` describe the previous design (claims, settlement ledger, idle USDC sleeve, `processWithdrawQueue` permissionless); they no longer match this branch.

## Things checked and found clean

- Reentrancy / ordering around spUSDC and USDC: all state updates precede the external calls (`delete` before `redeem`, `_burn` before `withdraw`); USDC and spUSDC have no hooks.
- Capacity invariant `totalSupply <= maxCapacity` after fills (fuzzed); `setCapacity` below `totalSupply` is tolerated by `availableCapacity()`.
- Loss while queued: escrowed redeem shares take `setChi` losses (intended); queued deposits sit in spUSDC and are unaffected.
- Fee cannot round to zero for a non-zero fee (R6) and cannot exceed 1% (`MAX_REDEEM_FEE`).
- Index monotonicity: `minVSR >= RAY` enforced, `setChi` only lowers, `_rpow` identical to sUSDS.
- No inflation / first-depositor vector (index pricing).
- `requestDeposit` instant path correctly resets `depositHead` only when `pendingDepositSavingsShares == 0`, which implies every earlier entry is filled or cancelled.
